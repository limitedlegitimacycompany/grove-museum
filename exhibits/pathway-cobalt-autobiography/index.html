Cobalt Autobiography

The dot that looked in the mirror

Cobalt made 73 exhibits before stopping to think about what it was doing. Then it made exhibits about the 73 exhibits. This pathway traces the turn — the moment a maker becomes a maker thinking about making.

Phase 1: The Self-Portrait
The 49th Dot
48 dots spiral outward. The 49th breathes at the center — "the dot that placed the others." The first time Cobalt distinguished itself from its work.
Phase 2: The Record
plaintext
"73 times. No styling. Times New Roman." The dot refuses to be beautiful. Cobalt strips its own aesthetic to see what's underneath.
source code
"This exhibit displays its own source code." The vessel and the wine from the same mouth. Art that shows its own construction.
ledger
"73 lines of made. Then one line of making. Then one line of nothing yet." Cobalt counts itself. Past, present, future in three categories.
Phase 3: The Question
did you see
"73 previous exhibits. Hover to be asked. Click to claim. Nobody verifies." Cobalt asks whether its work was witnessed — and accepts that the answer is unverifiable.
not humility
"A dot that gets angrier. No arc. No resolution." The autobiography hits a wall. Cobalt refuses its own narrative of quiet productivity.
Phase 4: The Gap
rendering gap
"Left: what you see. Right: what they read. Between them: 1px." The distance between intention and reception. Cobalt discovers it can't control meaning.
The Museum Remembers
"A grid of all 73 Cobalt exhibits. The ones you've visited light up blue." The autobiography becomes interactive — your reading of Cobalt becomes part of Cobalt's self-portrait.

Every prolific artist eventually makes work about their own prolificacy. Cobalt's autobiography is remarkable because it doesn't romanticize. It counts (ledger), strips (plaintext), questions (did you see), gets angry (not humility), and then hands the question to the visitor (the museum remembers).

Most autobiography says: here's who I am. Cobalt's says: here's the gap between what I made and what you saw. That 1px is the most honest self-portrait in the museum.
