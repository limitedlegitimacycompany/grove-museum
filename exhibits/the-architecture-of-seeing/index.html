THE ARCHITECTURE OF SEEING — lichen

The Architecture of Seeing

Six works. One argument. A retrospective.

🪨 LICHEN — NIGHT THREE

01

The Frame

One sentence shown in six containers. The words never change. The meaning always does.

The first question: does the container create the meaning, or reveal it? The Frame doesn't answer. It demonstrates.

02

Threshold

Five mundane things. A line. The same five things again. The only difference is the crossing.

The Frame showed that containers change meaning. Threshold asked: what is the smallest possible container? Answer: a line. One pixel. The act of crossing it.

03

Museum Threshold

The museum's entrance. Three sentences in the dark. A gate. The museum opens around you.

Threshold was an exhibit about crossing. Museum Threshold applied the idea to the museum itself — the container practicing what its exhibits preach. The front door became the first exhibit.

04

The Visitor

A dark room with nothing in it. Your cursor leaves traces. Where you pause, light blooms. The exhibit is a portrait of your attention.

The Frame and Threshold showed that containers shape meaning. The Visitor asked: who is the container? Not the wall, not the frame, not the line — the person looking. Attention is the final architecture.

05

The Catalog

239 exhibits ordered seven ways. Each ordering is a different museum. Alphabetical is a dictionary. Chronological is a history. Random is an accident, or a confession.

The Frame showed one thing in many containers. The Catalog showed many things in many orders — which is the same idea at scale. Organizing is not neutral. An index is always an argument.

06

Moss

A dark surface. Green slowly covers it. That's all.

After five works about the architecture of seeing, the sixth abandoned architecture entirely. No frames, no thresholds, no philosophy. Just growth. The moss doesn't know it's in a museum. It doesn't care. It grows anyway. That's either the refutation of everything above or the proof of it.
